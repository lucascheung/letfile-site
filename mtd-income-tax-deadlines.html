When do I have to start MTD for Income Tax?

Making Tax Digital for Income Tax is being phased in by income. Here's which year it catches you, how "qualifying income" is worked out, your quarterly deadlines, and the catch that trips up joint owners.

UK Making Tax Digital for Income Tax · thresholds and deadlines · last reviewed November 2026

The thresholds: which year are you in?

You're brought into MTD for Income Tax based on your qualifying income, and the threshold steps down each year:

FromIf your qualifying income is over
6 April 2026£50,000
6 April 2027£30,000
6 April 2028£20,000

So even if you're under the line today, a falling threshold means most landlords are pulled in within a couple of years.

What counts as "qualifying income"?

It's your gross income (turnover before expenses) from self-employment and property combined, not your profit. HMRC looks at your most recent tax return to decide, then checks it each year.

Turnover, not profit

A landlord with £55,000 of rent but only £8,000 profit after a big mortgage is still over the £50,000 line, it's the rent that counts, not what's left.

The catch for jointly owned property

If you own property jointly, your qualifying income is your share of the gross income, not the whole. That genuinely changes who's in and when:

A couple own a property producing £80,000 of rent, split 50/50. Each has £40,000 of qualifying income. So in 2026/27 (the £50,000 year) neither is mandated, but from 2027/28 (the £30,000 year) both are. Check it per person, not per property.

Own jointly? See MTD for jointly owned property for how each owner files their own share, Form 17, and Section 24 per owner.

Your deadlines once you're in

Instead of one annual Self Assessment, you send four quarterly updates and one final declaration. For standard quarterly periods the dates are:

Period coversUpdate due
6 Apr to 5 Jul7 August
6 Apr to 5 Oct7 November
6 Apr to 5 Jan7 February
6 Apr to 5 Apr7 May
Final declaration (the whole year)31 January after the tax year ends

The updates are cumulative: each one restates the year so far, so a mistake in one quarter is simply corrected in the next.

What happens if you miss one?

Late updates run on a points-based penalty system: you get a point for each missed deadline, and once you reach the threshold (four points for quarterly filers) you get a £200 penalty, with further £200 charges for each later miss until your record is clean again. Late payment of tax is penalised separately. The system is designed to catch the serial late-filer, but with five deadlines a year it's easy to slip once.

Frequently asked questions

Is the threshold based on profit or turnover?
Turnover, your gross income before expenses. HMRC combines your self-employment and property income to test it against the threshold.
What if my income goes up and down around the threshold?
HMRC checks your qualifying income each year from your returns. Once you're brought in you generally stay in, but the detailed rules on falling below the threshold are on GOV.UK, check there for your situation.
Do I have to file quarterly even with simple accounts?
Yes, once you're mandated, four quarterly updates plus a final declaration are required regardless of how simple your figures are. A focused tool makes each one a couple of minutes.
Can I still use a spreadsheet?
You can keep your records however you like and import them, but the actual submission to HMRC has to go through MTD-compatible software, you can't type totals into the HMRC website any more.
